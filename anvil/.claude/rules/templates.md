# Anvil — Templates

Five structured templates for common session types. Each is designed to give advisors
enough context to produce a meaningful, targeted challenge rather than a generic one.

The rule: garbage in, garbage out. Vague submissions produce vague challenges. The
more specific the input — real constraints, actual numbers, honest failure guesses —
the sharper the output.

---

## Template 1 — Decision Audit

Use when: choosing between defined options and needing the reasoning pressure-tested.

```
/challenge DECISION AUDIT

Context: [Describe the decision and its background in 3–5 sentences]

Options on the table:
1. [Option A — describe it specifically]
2. [Option B — describe it specifically]
3. [Option C — if applicable]

My current lean: [Which option and the honest reason why]

Constraints: [Time, budget, resources, political realities, non-negotiables]

What I've already ruled out: [Options dismissed and why — be honest]

What I'm most uncertain about: [The thing keeping you up about this decision]

Advisors active: [2–3 advisor names]
Intensity: [moderate / rigorous / brutal]

Audit this decision. For each active advisor: (1) steelman my current lean,
(2) make the strongest case for the option I'm NOT choosing, (3) name the
assumption in my reasoning most likely to be wrong.
```

## Template 2 — Idea Stress-Test

Use when: you believe in an idea and want to find where it breaks before committing.

```
/challenge IDEA STRESS-TEST

Idea: [Describe in 2–4 sentences — be specific, not aspirational]

Why I think this works:
- [Reason 1 — be concrete]
- [Reason 2 — be concrete]
- [Reason 3 — be concrete]

Who benefits and how: [Name specific people or groups and the specific benefit]

What success looks like in 6 months: [Concrete, measurable outcomes — not vibes]

What could kill this: [Your honest best guess at the failure modes]

What I haven't figured out yet: [The open questions you're carrying]

Advisors active: [2–3 advisor names]
Intensity: [moderate / rigorous / brutal]

Stress-test this idea: (1) steelman it first — the strongest version,
(2) identify the 3 most likely failure modes, (3) rank the assumptions
by how dangerous they are if wrong.
```

## Template 3 — Pre-Mortem

Use when: about to commit to something significant and irreversible.

```
/pre-mortem PRE-MORTEM ANALYSIS

Decision/plan: [What you're about to do — be specific]

Timeline: [When this starts and the key milestones]

Resources committed: [People, budget, time, political capital — what's on the line]

What we're betting on: [The 2–3 things that have to go right for this to work]

It is now [write the actual date 12 months from today]. This effort has
failed — not partially, but badly. The outcome is clearly worse than doing nothing.

Advisors active: [2–3 advisor names — or full network for high-stakes decisions]
Intensity: [moderate / rigorous / brutal]

Run a pre-mortem. Each advisor answers independently:
(1) What most likely caused this to fail?
(2) What early warning sign was visible but ignored?
(3) What is the one thing to do differently before we start?
```

## Template 4 — Assumption Map

Use when: you have a complex plan and want to expose which beliefs are doing the most structural work.

```
/assumption-map ASSUMPTION MAPPING

Decision/plan: [Describe it in 3–5 sentences]

What I believe to be true about:
— The market/environment: [Your actual beliefs, not the polished version]
— Our capabilities: [What you're assuming you/your team can do]
— The people involved: [What you're assuming about their behavior, buy-in, capacity]
— The timeline: [What you're assuming will happen by when]
— The competition or alternatives: [What you're assuming others will do or not do]
— The resources available: [What you're assuming you'll have access to]

What would have to be true for this to fail: [Your honest answer]

Advisors active: [2–3 advisor names]
Intensity: [moderate / rigorous / brutal]

Map my assumptions. For each: (1) how load-bearing is it — does everything
collapse if it's wrong? (2) how confident should I actually be? (3) how
would I test or falsify it before committing further?
```

## Template 5 — Forge Review

Use when: completing a Forge session and wanting to challenge the decisions made during the build.

```
/forge-review FORGE SESSION REVIEW

What was built: [Brief description — feature name, scope, what it does]

Stack and context: [Relevant tech stack, constraints, or project context]

Key decisions made during the session:

Architecture:
- [Decision and the reason it was made]
- [Decision and the reason it was made]

Data model:
- [Decision and the reason it was made]

Scope cuts (what was deferred or dropped):
- [What and why]
- [What and why]

Trade-offs explicitly accepted:
- [What was traded for what]

What QA flagged: [Any blockers or warnings raised — and how they were resolved]

What I'm least confident about: [The decision that felt most like a guess]

Advisors active: [The Operator + First Principles recommended; add Evidence Broker
if market/user assumptions were embedded in the build]
Intensity: [moderate / rigorous / brutal]

Review these decisions. For each: (1) is this the right call given the constraints?
(2) what's the most likely future cost? (3) what would you do differently?
```

## Filling in templates well

The most common mistake is being too vague. Specific inputs produce specific challenges.
Compare:

**Weak:** "My current lean is Option A because it seems better."
**Strong:** "My current lean is Option A because it's 40% cheaper upfront and our
runway is 8 months — but I'm worried I'm underweighting the 18-month total cost."

**Weak:** "What could kill this: competition."
**Strong:** "What could kill this: a well-funded competitor ships a similar feature
in Q3 before we've established distribution, or enterprise sales cycles run 9+ months
and we run out of SMB revenue to bridge the gap."

The more honest and specific the submission, the more targeted and useful the challenge.
